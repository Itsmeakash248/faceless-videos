## 2. Description

In September 1971, Lloyds Bank on Baker Street believed its 20-ton steel vault door was physically unbreachable. 

So a London gang never touched it.

Instead, they rented a leather goods shop two doors down, dropped 5 feet into the cellar, and hand-dug a 40-foot horizontal tunnel underneath the Chicken Inn restaurant—hauling out 8 long tons of dirt by hand. Rising 15 feet directly beneath the bank, they hit 3 feet of reinforced concrete. 

When a 100-ton hydraulic jack sank into an ancient unmapped well, and a 3,000°C thermal lance choked the tunnel in toxic smoke, they packed gelignite into drilled holes, timed the detonation to passing traffic, and blasted a 12-by-14 inch hole straight through the floor. 

268 safe-deposit boxes were emptied. £1.5 million vanished. Half a mile away, an amateur radio operator accidentally intercepted their walkie-talkie transmissions across London (*"Money may be your God, but it's not mine"*). Police searched 750 banks, but only checked the front doors.
